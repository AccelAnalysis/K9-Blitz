# ADR-0002 — Owner-Authorized Digital Rules May Fill Legacy Source Gaps

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-13
- **Decision owner:** K9 Blitz product owner
- **Supersedes:** the blanket interpretation that every gameplay gap must remain unresolved until a legacy physical source is recovered

## Context

The K9 Blitz repository was initially bootstrapped from board imagery and partial descriptive materials. Early guardrails correctly prevented implementation lanes from silently guessing undocumented physical-game rules and then presenting those guesses as recovered facts.

The product owner has now explicitly authorized the digital game to define any gameplay details that are unavailable from the legacy source materials and has confirmed ownership/control of the game and artwork.

Keeping every missing legacy detail permanently unresolved would therefore block product completion even though the owner has authority to design the digital edition.

## Decision

K9 Blitz will maintain two distinct kinds of rule provenance:

1. **Source-backed legacy fact** — a physical-game rule/component behavior supported by an authoritative source such as a rulebook, card, token, player aid, or approved asset.
2. **Owner-authored digital rule** — behavior intentionally designed for the digital product where source material is silent or incomplete.

An owner-authored digital rule is authoritative once it is:

- explicitly specified;
- assigned to a rules/content version;
- committed to the repository;
- implemented behind the authoritative game-state/rules boundary; and
- covered by appropriate tests.

It must not be described as a recovered historical/physical rule unless evidence later establishes that equivalence.

## Initial authority

`docs/DIGITAL_RULES_V1.md` establishes **K9 Blitz Digital Rules v1.0** as the launch digital rules authority.

The launch rules include owner-authored decisions for player count, turn order, two-die movement, overshoot behavior, Paw Token semantics, Competition Track progression, Trainer Card effects, non-cascading card movement, hidden-information policy, and first-to-Finish victory.

## Architectural consequences

The existing core invariants remain unchanged:

- one authoritative game state;
- commands/intents in, state/events out;
- presentation does not decide correctness;
- random outcomes are generated by the authoritative runtime appropriate to the game mode;
- revisions protect against stale concurrent commands;
- saved games retain their rules/content versions.

This ADR changes **where rule authority may come from**, not **where rule execution lives**.

## QA consequences

QA must evaluate a rule against its declared provenance:

- a legacy-fidelity claim fails/blocks if required source evidence is absent;
- an owner-authored digital rule is testable against its committed specification and is not blocked merely because a legacy source never defined it;
- material rule changes require a version/provenance update.

## Future reconciliation

If later-discovered physical materials conflict with an owner-authored digital rule, the team may:

- keep the digital rule as a deliberate digital-edition difference;
- create a compatibility/classic rules mode; or
- supersede the digital rule in a new version.

Existing saved games must never be silently reinterpreted under the new version.
